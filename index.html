    Wherein St. Thomas Aquinas, drawing upon Scripture and the Fathers, unfolds the five qualities of prayer and expounds, petition by petition, the words Our Lord Himself taught His disciples to say.

    Begin the Reading
    Descend the Folio

    "Among all other prayers, the Lord's Prayer holds the chief place." So begins this exposition, in which five marks of true prayer — that it be confident, ordered, suitable, devout, and humble — are drawn out before the seven petitions themselves are opened, one by one.

    Recapitulation

    A Short Explanation of the Whole Prayer

    Brevis Summa Totius Orationis

    The Lord's Prayer contains all that we ought to desire, and all that we ought to avoid. Of all desirable things, that must be most desired which is most loved — and that is God. Four goods are therefore sought, and four evils are put to flight.

    I. The Glory of God
    Hallowed be Thy Name

    Sought first of all, for it is most loved. No evil is contrary to it — sin and virtue alike redound, at last, to God's glory.

    II. Eternal Life
    Thy Kingdom Come

    Contrary to it is sin, by which eternal life is lost; so we pray also, Forgive us our trespasses.

    III. Justice & Good Works
    Thy Will Be Done

    Contrary to it is temptation, which hinders good; so we pray, Lead us not into temptation.

    IV. The Necessaries of Life
    Give Us This Day

    Contrary to it are trouble and adversity; so we pray at the last, But deliver us from evil.

    The Prayer Entire

    Our Father, who art in Heaven,
    Hallowed be Thy Name.
    Thy Kingdom come.
    Thy Will be done on earth as it is in Heaven.
    Give us this day our daily bread.
    And forgive us our trespasses,
    as we forgive those who trespass against us.
    And lead us not into temptation,
    but deliver us from evil. Amen.
